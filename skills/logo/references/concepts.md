# Concept starters

Use these when the user has no direction. Pick 6–8 that fit the product and
describe each in one or two sentences.

## Monogram / letterform

Build the mark from the initials or the first letter. Works when the name is
short and distinctive. Avoid when the letter is common or the result looks like a
stock letter icon.

## Negative space

Hide a second meaning inside a solid shape. Works for clever, premium brands.
Avoid when the hidden shape needs explanation or disappears at 16 px.

## Containment / frame

Put the idea inside a portal, window, bracket or tile. Works for tools, editors
and platforms. Avoid when the container is more memorable than the content.

## Flow / convergence

Several paths, particles or lines converge into one. Works for orchestration,
pipelines, setup and integration. Avoid five thin lines that turn to mush at
small sizes.

## Grid / module

A mark assembled from squares or cells, with one cell highlighted. Works for
composable systems, builders and libraries. Avoid generic four-square grids.

## Object metaphor

Use a physical object: buckle, clip, loom, compass, key, lever, crate. Works when
the metaphor is immediate and ownable. Avoid literal drawings with too much
detail.

## Stack / layers

Overlapping planes show composition and reuse. Works for platforms, kits and
skill libraries. Avoid when the layers look like a generic database icon.

## Signal / waveform

A controlled pulse or wave suggests response, sync or intelligence. Works for
agents, automation and observability. Avoid when it reads as a generic audio
icon.

## Knot / weave

Interlaced strands suggest integration and a shared source of truth. Works for
harnesses, orchestrators and networks. Avoid when the knot becomes noise at
small sizes.

## Compass / direction

A pointer or cardinal star suggests guidance and setup. Works for onboarding and
developer tools. Avoid generic arrows and paper planes.

## Spark / state change

A small burst or cut marks a transformation, build or release. Works for
compilers, generators and release tools. Avoid literal lightning bolts.

## Wordmark-led

Make the name the hero with a distinctive cut, ligature or spacing. Works when
the name is short and the typography is ownable. Avoid when the name is long or
the font cannot be licensed.

## Choosing

| Product trait | Strong starters |
| --- | --- |
| Orchestration, setup | Flow, knot, frame, buckle |
| Library, composability | Grid, stack, module |
| Tooling, precision | Monogram, negative space, object |
| Agents, automation | Signal, spark, flow |
| Premium, editorial | Wordmark, monogram, negative space |
| Playful | Object, spark, containment |
